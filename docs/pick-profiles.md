# Pick profiles: how the shapes are built

The own-design picks are generated by code (`build/pick_shapes.py`, used by `build/generate_home_workshop.py`,
`build/generate_tension_tools.py` and the customiser). They are parametric, not traced from any maker's tool, and
they follow the shapes of standard commercial profiles. None has been tested on a real lock yet.

## What changed after review (2026-11-26)

A reviewer pointed out three faults in the first set. All three were right.

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hooks were right angles | Each hook was a square post stuck on a straight shaft | Hooks are now a bent strip: the shaft curves up on a bend radius and narrows to a 0.9 mm rounded tip. No corner anywhere |
| Rakes made no sense | Bumps sat on top of a flat bar, and the tip ended in a square face | Bogota and snake rakes are a wavy strip (both edges follow the wave). City, triple-peak and zig-zag rakes end in a sloped nose |
| Combs could not reach the shear line | 2.2 mm teeth on a 4.6 mm body | 5 mm teeth on a 2 mm body, a stop for the lock face, teeth at the lock's real spacing. Sums below |

The curved hooks, lifters and wave rake were dropped: the swept-hook range covers them.

## Hooks

A centreline runs along the shaft, turns up on an arc of radius R, then runs straight to the tip. The strip narrows
from the shaft height (1.2 mm) to 0.9 mm and ends round. "Tip height" is from the top of the shaft to the top of the tip.

| Hook | Tip height | Bend radius | Lean forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short | 2.0 mm | 1.6 mm | 10° |
| Medium | 3.0 mm | 1.8 mm | 8° |
| Deep | 4.2 mm | 2.0 mm | 6° |
| Offset | 3.0 mm | 1.8 mm | 28° |
| Swept range | 1.2–5.0 mm | one long arc, reach 0.8 × height + 1.6 mm | — |

## Rakes and half-diamonds

| Profile | Shape |
|---|---|
| Bogota 2 / 3 | Strip with 2 or 3 humps over 9 / 13.5 mm, humps 1.65 mm above the shaft |
| Snake | Strip waving up and down, 4 humps over 12 mm |
| City, triple-peak | Solid profile on top of the shaft, front slopes down to the bottom edge |
| Half-diamonds | Back slope up to the peak, front slope straight to the bottom edge (pointed nose) |

## Combs

A comb pushes every pin stack up until the key pins are wholly above the shear line, then the plug turns.

- **Shear line.** Kwikset: a #1 cut is 0.329" above the key's bottom edge and takes a 0.172" key pin, so the shear
  line is 0.501" (12.7 mm) above the bottom of the keyway ([LockWiki](https://www.lockwiki.com/index.php/Kwikset_Classic)).
- **Keyway ceiling.** Between the pin holes the keyway is about as tall as an uncut key, about 8.5 mm.
- **Use.** Slide the comb in along the floor of the keyway, under the resting pins, until the stop touches the lock
  face. Lift it: the body rises until it meets the ceiling, and the teeth go up the pin holes.
- **Sizes.** Body 2.0 mm + teeth 5.0 mm = 7.0 mm, low enough to slide under the pins. Lifted, the tooth tops reach
  8.5 + 5.0 = 13.5 mm, 0.8 mm past the shear line. The old 2.2 mm teeth reached only 10.7 mm.
- **Alignment.** Teeth are 1.6 mm wide; pin holes are about 2.9 mm. The stop puts the first tooth at the first-cut
  distance: Kwikset 6.27 mm, spacing 3.81 mm (LockWiki); Schlage 5.87 mm, spacing 3.96 mm (commonly published,
  not checked here: measure your key).
- **Limits.** Locks with anti-comb (overlift) protection, or with too little room above the pins, cannot be combed.
  The 8.5 mm ceiling is an estimate: if your comb will not slide in, file the teeth down a little.
